# Stage Build Unit Costs

Factory Town Phase 1 reported **$13,075,392 against a $13,376,463 CER — 2.25% under budget.** Inside that, the two headline stage structures came in at **2.71x**: Infinity at $3,300,297 against $1,500,000, Park at $2,129,350 against $500,000. A **$3,429,647** structure overrun was absorbed almost entirely by **$2,835,000** of sound, power, SFX and HVAC lines that were carried at full value and never spent at all.

That is the lesson before any rate below is used: **a stage budget's total tells you nothing. Only the lines with a quantity under them do.**

## The delivered stages

Forecast at 10/15/25, a mix of paid invoices and open commitments. Capacities from the Factory Town show model.

| Stage | Cap | What the roof does | Delivered | $/cap |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Infinity | 8,500 | Open air, **crowd** weather protection | $4,104,314 | $483 |
| Park | 6,000 | Covers **performers and equipment only** | $2,633,142 | $439 |
| Warehouse #4 | 2,000 | Existing building, structural retrofit | $1,630,330 | $815 |
| Chain Room | 2,000 | Existing building, column and beam work | $523,482 | $262 |
| Cypress End | 750 | Aux stage, 42' x 28' x 28' + portal | $78,057 | $104 |

**Every one of these excludes sound, power and SFX**, which were budgeted and never bought in Phase 1. Add them before comparing to anything. And read the $/cap column with the roof column next to it: Infinity costs more per head than Park almost entirely because its roof has to stand over the audience.

**Small rooms cost more per head, not less.** Warehouse #4 at $815/cap is nearly eight times Cypress End at $104/cap, because fixture counts, consoles, distro and structural minimums do not scale down with capacity. Never price a small room by discounting a large one.

## Structure is the line that breaks

It broke on both stages and it will break on yours. Price it as three separate things, because they behave differently:

**Fabricated structure.** Infinity's stage design and fabrication ran **$2,163,275** (structure, video support and FOH platform in one line, Mountain Production). Park's space frame ran **$1,534,079** plus **$519,986** of further stage work.

**What is in the ground.** Infinity footers were **$615,642** plus a **$211,354** change order — **$826,996, or 25% of that stage's entire structure line.** Park's civil ground work totalled **$239,896** (rebar $82,400 + $29,000, concrete footers $60,000, the balance heavy equipment and labour). Foundations are the least transferable number in this whole file and the most likely to carry a change order.

**Erection.** Crane rental $45,000 on Infinity. Stage build labour $122,716 (Island Productions), against a whole-project labour contract of $149,510. Heavy equipment rental appears in four separate cost codes across the two stages — consolidate it or you will double-count it.

## Lighting: price the fixture, then price the install separately

Delivered fixture rates:

| Item | Qty | Cost | Unit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Chauvet Colorstrike M (Infinity) | 48 | $118,616 | **$2,471 / fixture** |
| Site lighting effects fixtures, Phase 2 | 50 | $32,100 | **$642 / fixture** |
| Architectural fixtures (Studio Lux) | — | $160,260 | package |
| Chain Room lighting package (LPS) | — | $319,116 | $172 / cap |

**Install is a separate number and its ratio is not constant.** In-room at Warehouse #4, install ran **16.7%** of fixture purchase ($72,607 on $434,323). Across the site it ran **54.4%** on the same basis, and **67.5%** once the electricians are added ($209,760 on $310,807). The variable is cable distance, not fixture count. Anyone quoting a single install percentage for a whole project is guessing.

## Video: one buy, two stages, and the steel is somebody else's line

The LED purchase was a **single $655,396 order from one vendor**, split Infinity $415,575 / Park $231,796 / tax $8,025. Buying both stages' walls in one order is the reason that number is as low as it is — and it is the single easiest saving to reproduce.

**What holds the wall up is priced by the stage fabricator, not the LED vendor.** Park video support was $24,075 to Mountain Production; Infinity's is buried inside the $2,163,275 fabrication line. An LED quote that looks complete and carries no support structure is missing the expensive half. Ask for it explicitly.

## Barricade

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Black crash barricade, 200 LF | $40,151 — **$200.76 / LF** |
| Freight | $5,377 — **13.4%** on top, so **$227.64 / LF delivered** |
| Corner pieces (two orders) | $16,773 |
| Second run, Infinity & Warehouse, incl. freight | $26,000 |
| **Package total** | **$88,301** against an $80,000 budget |

**Barricade is quoted by the foot and bought by the corner.** Corners were **19% of the delivered package** and they are not in the per-foot rate. Count them off the plan — every stage front, every pit return, every gate — before accepting a linear-foot quote. Freight is a second line and it is never included.

The aesthetic wall costs **5x** the temporary panel per linear foot. That is a real choice with a real number on it, and it should be made on the drawings rather than discovered in an invoice.

**A quote for one bar is not a rate for the next one.** Bar #22 was estimated at $24,000 off Bar #61's quote and landed at **$28,250 — 17.7% over**. Bar #61 itself took a $4,250 extension charge on a $36,000 fabrication.

## The soft costs that ride every stage

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Structural engineering | **0.67%** of structure (Infinity, $22,060) to **1.50%** (Park, $31,920) |
| Private provider plan review + inspections | **$13,500 per stage** ($7,500 + $6,000), each |
| Expediting, per footer permit | $1,500 |
| Private provider footer structure review | $20,300 (Infinity) |
| Creative production stage design | **$171,244 = 3.15%** of the two structures |
| Architect of record | $132,500 = **1.01%** of programme |

Structural engineering doubling between two stages on the same site is not a rate difference — it is a difference in how much of the design the engineer had to originate. Price it against the *definition* of the structure, not against its cost.

## Buy versus rent, by system and by room — never by site

The owner's own AV file settles this, and it does not settle it the way slogans do. Against a year of eight shows / sixteen festival days:

| System | Annual rental | Purchase | Breakeven |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Infinity lighting | $457,348 | $1,000,000 | **2.32 yr** |
| Park lighting | $222,785 | $500,000 | **2.38 yr** |
| Infinity audio | $218,722 | $750,000 | 3.84 yr |
| Park audio | $117,400 | $750,000 | 9.07 yr |
| Warehouse audio | $30,000 | $400,000 | **18.93 yr** |
| Whole site, all AV | $1,910,847 | $4,700,000 | 2.61 yr |

**Lighting buys itself in about two and a half years. Audio mostly does not.** The site-wide 2.61-year figure is real and it hides an 18.93-year line inside it — and Warehouse sound was bought anyway, for $407,246. Run the payback per system per room, and when a room's own arithmetic says rent, say so out loud rather than letting the blended number carry it.

## Moving these rates to 165 Randolph

These are Hialeah numbers: outdoor, industrial, private-provider permitting, Florida labour, no snow. 165 Randolph is an indoor Brooklyn conversion under DOB with a Place of Assembly. Sort every rate into what travels and what does not.

**Travels, with escalation only** — fixture unit prices, the LED package rate, barricade per foot, container fabrication, console and distro counts. These are national equipment markets.

**Does not travel** — foundations and footings (soil, water table and frost line are local, and Infinity's were 25% of its structure), install and erection labour, crane and heavy equipment, the permitting path and its fees, and anything sized by the roof. A Brooklyn roof carries snow load and usually rigging load; a Hialeah stage roof carries neither.

**Changes shape entirely** — hoisting and access. Every one of these stages was built in open air with a crane on the slab. Getting the same structure into an existing Brooklyn building is a different scope with no line in this file.

The one conclusion that does transfer intact: **ground-supported beats hung.** Every Factory Town stage stands on its own footings, which is why the roofs carry no rigging load and why the fabricated structure prices as low as it does per capacity. Test that before pricing a rigging grid anywhere.

## Never

Never quote a stage total that silently excludes sound and power — say what is not in it. Never price a small room by discounting a large one. Never accept an LED quote without the support structure that holds it. Never take a barricade per-foot rate as the package price: add corners and freight. Never carry a foundation number across a city. Never let a blended site-wide payback authorise a purchase its own room cannot justify. Never treat a quote for one bar, one container or one stage as a rate for the next one — it is an estimate, and at Factory Town it ran 17.7% light.
