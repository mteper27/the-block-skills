### The roof lift is CONTESTED as of 16 Sep 2026

**The RFEM model does not lift anything.** 124 of 243 column members start at or
below +2 ft (62.4% of column steel); all 517 truss members are new between +56.5
and +64.5 ft; the "Existing Roof / Lower" level carries zero members; the words
lift, jack, shore and splice appear nowhere in 8,658 member rows. The structural
engineer and the Master Development Budget are both building a REBUILD to
+64 ft 6 in.

The section below is our prior position. It has NOT been abandoned — Matthew has

### Our prior position: the roof lift is MANDATORY

It is not an option, not a scenario, not a value-engineering candidate. The 50 ft
clear height is the moat — column-free industrial buildings over 50K SF are
extremely rare in NYC, and the 12-18 month DOB structural approval is itself the
barrier that protects the position once built. **Every scenario includes it.**

Roof lift quote (Matthew + Eric): **$3–4M**, carried at $3.5M. That is the
LIFTER'S SCOPE ONLY. Surrounding work — sidewalk shed, MEP off and back on, roof
repair, fireproofing, insulation, fire separation, permits — is separate and adds
roughly $3.5M more.

**A roof lift keeps the existing roof.** You cut it free of the perimeter walls,
jack it up, extend the columns underneath, and clad the new band of wall. You do
NOT demolish the roof and rebuild at height — that is a different, far more
expensive operation, and the development budget wrongly assumed it.

### Stage and mezzanine are GROUND-UP

Both stand on their own footings poured into the slab, Factory Town Infinity
style. Nothing hangs from the building. This is what removes the rigging load
from the roof and lets roof steel stay at ~15 lb/SF.

### Factory Town is a comp, with two adjustments

FT Infinity is **open-air** and **Miami-Dade hurricane-rated** (~175 mph design
wind). Before applying any FT structure or foundation rate:
1. Strip the open-air stage roof and crowd weather protection — this building has a roof.
2. Strip the hurricane foundations — an interior Brooklyn structure carries no wind load.

FT Infinity actual structure + foundations was $3,506,289 against a $2.0M budget
(+75%). Use actuals, never FT's budget lines.

### House setup, not touring inventory

Every artist brings a different production. The venue provides a **house** PA
(always house — nobody tours a PA for this size room), house lighting positions
and a competent house rig, and the mounting structure and power for visiting
productions. LED walls are Phase 2 — artists bring or rent their own.

### The deal structure, as at 16 Sep 2026

**EBC owns the land. We sign a 30-year lease. Insomniac funds and builds out the
building.** This supersedes the earlier note that Live Nation / Insomniac is a
co-pro and programming partner only — Insomniac is now the capital behind the
build as well.

Three things follow from it and they must not be lost:

1. **Everything we build is a leasehold improvement.** The cost is amortised
   against a 30-year term, not against an owned freehold. At $77.9M that is
   roughly $2.6M a year of capital before any financing cost, and the
   improvements revert to EBC at the end of the term unless the lease says
   otherwise.
2. **Ask what EBC contributes.** In a build-to-suit on the landlord's land it is
   normal for the landlord to contribute — a work allowance, a rent-free period,
   a share of the structural scope, or a purchase option. Nobody has asked. This
   is the single largest untested lever on what the build costs US as opposed to
   what it costs.
3. **The lease dictates the insurance, not the contractor.** EBC will require to
   be named on the policies, and NY Labor Law 240 exposes the OWNER of the
   project regardless of who carries the cover. An owner-controlled insurance
   programme has to be written to satisfy the lease.

**Never assume the landlord pays for structure.** On a ground lease it is all
tenant work unless the lease says otherwise. Read the lease before moving any
line out of our budget.
